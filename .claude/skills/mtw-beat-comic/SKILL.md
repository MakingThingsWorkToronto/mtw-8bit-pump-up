# MTW Beat Comic

Read `discussion/spec.md` first (the "Homepage B", "Beat Comic", "mixtape" and 2026-09-27 paragraphs): it wins over this skill. The first Beat Comic is the homepage (`/`, Track 01); Agentic Systems is Track 02. Matt asked for this method so a song plus a story produces the same thing for every page and Journal entry (2026-09-26).

**The homepage is the benchmark** (Matt, 2026-09-27: "the home animation is your benchmark", "anchor the skill to home"). Every Beat Comic must be as dense, fast, funny and on the beat as the homepage's, and must look like it: the same chapter-card proportions, the same exact grids of equal cells, the same slab sizes, the same pace in seconds. When a new comic looks different from the homepage in any of these, the new comic (or the engine) is wrong. Before writing one, read [references/demo-anatomy.md](references/demo-anatomy.md) (both comics beat by beat) and open the homepage with music. Load the rest progressively: [story-craft](references/story-craft.md) while writing, [engine-reference](references/engine-reference.md) for exact timings, [lessons](references/lessons.md) before verifying.

**What it is.** An old-school comic fused into the site's pixel grid, told like a story round a campfire, cut to the beat. Flat brand colours only (ink, panel, teal, teal-light, paper, the teal band ground), no glass, nothing radial. Pages of panels land on the beat, each frame inked by a vector pen; images and type are pixelated and posterized to the flat palette; narration types into teal caption boxes; chapter cards flash on the teal banner; pixel SFX slabs hit on the song's accents and explode; embers rise off the grid; the world freezes when the music stops dead. It ends on the page's own heading resolving from 8-bit into modern type, and the first scroll, key or swipe, or Skip Intro, folds the stage into the page.

## The standard (every new comic; `beat-comic-check.mjs` enforces the starred items)

1. **A numbered track.*** `track: track('<page path>')` from `site/src/data/beat-comics/tracklist.ts`, which gives `{ disc, no, title }`. Two discs, each numbered from its own Track 01 (Matt, 2026-09-28: "journal should be a separate disk on the site playlist"): **Disc 1, the site** (19 tracks: the homepage 01, then each landing page followed by its services in the site's service order, then About Matt, 19) and **Disc 2, the Journal** (one track per published entry with a comic, in the order of the months the entries cover: 01 A second brain needs an owner to build it, 02 Agents build the tools they work with, 03 Standardize document generation quality with a rubric, 04 Reference code makes agent builds faster, 05 An agent's report is not evidence; a new entry is appended). A track's title is its page's own (a Journal track's is the entry's `title`; the audit checks it). The song comes from the soundtrack library, chosen for the page's character.
2. **The cover.** The homepage's layout: the MTW mark and `TRACK nn.` full width with a CD-player `ticker`* inside the main box (the song's time and "TRACK nn * TITLE" scrolling), the page title (`pix`, `small`) and a chat window with the tone line ("Sarcastic. Also serious.") below. The homepage alone adds the sound prompt (`sound: true`, `until`). **A section landing page** (`/agentic-systems/`, `/traditional-systems/`, `/data-migrations/`; Matt, 2026-09-28) swaps the two: the section name scrolls as an 8-bit marquee beside the mark in the main box (`pix` + `scroll: true`, the ticker kept inside) and `TRACK nn.` sits in the bottom-left box*; both from the tracklist, never retyped. A service page keeps the homepage's layout. **A Journal entry** (Disc 2) keeps the service layout with its disc in the main box, `DISC 2. TRACK nn.` (`pix: \`DISC ${tr.disc}. TRACK ${tr.no}.\``), and the entry's title small below; its ticker reads "DISC 2 * TRACK nn * TITLE" by itself (the engine adds the disc after Disc 1; Disc 1's covers are unchanged). **The homepage's own cover is the mixtape's back cover** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "restyle the first scene on homepage to reflect the tracklist you can greenfield that"): the mark and MAKING THINGS WORK. on top (no ticker: the tracklist is its CD display), the tracklist panel (`tracks: { rows: playlistRows('/'), live: 10 }`) in the middle, the sound box below until 9.5; the rows land like a player reading the disc, Track 01 waits cued on the dead stop and plays on the drop (lit, the play head blinking, the song's time), when every row becomes a link and PRACTICAL AI SOLUTIONS. scrolls where the sound box was; MATT STARK. on 12. The tracklist's links go live only after the sound box leaves*.
3. **The story.** One story in chapters, with its own contrast (the big-firm chapter is the homepage's alone*); sarcastic and serious in turns, one chapter only serious, on the song's dead stop. Claims only from cleared copy. **No slam word twice*** and **no "press start" anywhere***. A running joke gets one strong moment ("play down the feed language").
4. **The pace, in seconds.** About 1.5 s a panel and 5-7 s a page whatever the tempo (a 77 BPM song: a panel every 2 beats, 8-beat pages, 4-beat gags); a slam on most accents; freezes on the dead stops*.
5. **The look, as the homepage.** Grids are exact cells (thirds, quarters), never overlapping*; chapter titles short (they are fitted to one line); a slam still up when the next card flashes sits high or low*; figures may count up while their scene holds (`count`). **One image kind per scene***: a page's image panels are all cinematic photos (`photo`, `cover`) or all holograms (`hero`, `contain`), following the scene's dominant kind (Matt, 2026-09-28: "if the basic is a cinematic image then all boxes should use that"); pix, chart and chat panels do not count. **A box with a label is a cinematic photo**: every image panel with a caption or a name bar uses `card('<service>')` (the service's overview-card photo) or `photo('<starting point>')`, never a hologram, which sits letterboxed small in its box with the label over most of it (Matt, 2026-09-28: "some box images are hidden behind labels because we use the holo not the cinematic, so we ne never see it"); a hologram only in a box with no label. No picture twice in a scene. `node scripts/beat-comic-audit.mjs` (from `site/`) checks it across every comic. The engine makes every image shimmer and keeps it still (automatic).
6. **The ending.*** `closeScene` repeats the page hero's own words (the heading `lines`, and the `lede` and hero `image` on a service page); `closeScene.pixelate` is **the close through the ages** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "take us from 8 bit to common pixels to 2026 pixels, this is an animation through the ages of pixelation", "fade the section image in AFTER the font clarifies to beauty text"): the heading lands in big boxy 8-bit pixels and, from that beat of the close (5 in a 12-beat close, 4 in 10, 3 in 8), resolves in clean stages a beat apart (16-bit, the familiar pixels of a desktop screen, then the page's own smooth type); the hero image (or the homepage's panel) fades in only after that. The engine does all of it; a story sets only the beat. **A full-bleed hero** (the image edge to edge behind the heading: a Journal entry, About Matt) sets `closeScene.layout: 'full-bleed'`*: the close lays the page hero's image in the page hero's own box with its crop and gradient, and the heading and lede in the page title's place at its size, so the fold lands on the same picture (the audit fails a Journal or About close without it, and a side-image page with it). `holdEnd` with `holdLive` holds there with the embers rising. No "TO BE CONTINUED." and no High Scores (both retired 2026-09-27).
7. **Sound, automatic.** Music on by default, the credit tag, the site's one sound setting shared with the nav's Sound Toggle, and the DJ crossfade between pages are the engine's; a story adds nothing for them.
8. **The motion toolbar and the Tracklist, automatic** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "give the user a button similar to "Skip Intro" to open tracklist on each page reveal, this will be the perm way to stay in the 8-bit experience", then "remove "Pause" text from button, add next and prev track icon only buttons this toolbar shall be canonical in all motion graphic experiences"). Every comic carries the one toolbar (`MotionToolbar.astro`): left to right Let's Talk (8-bit, to `/contact/`), Tracklist, Previous track, Pause/Play, Next track (those three icon only), Skip Intro and the music tag; after the fold Let's Talk, Tracklist, Previous, Next and the tag stay pinned bottom right. Tracklist opens the site playlist (`playlist.ts`, the homepage cover's rows, this page's track playing) as an 8-bit dialog: **the comics' global navigation**, in the landing marquee's style. A story adds nothing for it.

## The motion toolbar (canonical; Matt, 2026-09-28)

One toolbar for every motion graphic experience: the Beat Comics now, Comic B and Game C next. Its state lives in one module (`motion-toolbar.js`) so a later persistent player (Astro's ClientRouter with a music deck) can keep it across page swaps.

- **Order**: Let's Talk, Tracklist, Previous track, Pause/Play, Next track, Skip Intro, then the player's music tag (the component's slot). Pinned after the fold: Let's Talk, Tracklist, Previous, Next, the tag (Pause/Play and Skip Intro leave with the experience; the tag mutes the music from then on). A phone shows icons only, except Let's Talk's pixel words, within 390 px.
- **Previous and Next** are real links to the tracks either side of the page's own in the whole playlist, Disc 1 then Disc 2 (the page transition and the DJ crossfade apply), named "Previous track: <title>" and "Next track: <title>" (aria-label and title). They cross the discs and wrap round (2026-09-28): Disc 1 Track 19's Next is Disc 2 Track 01, Disc 2 Track 05's Next is Disc 1 Track 01, and Disc 1 Track 01's Previous is the last Journal track, as a mixtape plays on; on a Journal entry they step through the Journal's tracks. From a page that is not a track, Next is Disc 1 Track 01 and Previous the last track.
- **Pause/Play** is icon only; its name (aria-label, title and a visually hidden label) says what a press does next.
- **Adopting it (Comic B's `ComicStory.astro` and `comic-story.js`, Game C):** render `<MotionToolbar>` inside the experience's stage (pass the credit tag markup as its child if the experience plays music; pass `current="/"` on a `-b` page so the track it stands for plays in the Tracklist and Previous and Next step from it; `skip={false}` or `pause={false}` if it has no such action) and place `.rl-dock` with the experience's own CSS, without restyling the buttons. The component loads `motion-toolbar.js` before the experience's script. In the script: `MTWToolbar.register({ beat, songTime, togglePause, skip })` (seconds a beat; the song's time; what Pause/Play and Skip Intro do); call `MTWToolbar.setPaused(state)` whenever it pauses or resumes; ignore any input that would advance, fold or leave while `MTWToolbar.isOpen()` (the Tracklist is open); call `MTWToolbar.pin(tagElement)` when it folds into the page. Instead of callbacks it may listen for the window event `mtw-toolbar` (`detail.action`: pause, play, skip, prev, next, tracklist-open, tracklist-close). Let's Talk, Tracklist, Previous and Next need nothing from it. Do not copy the toolbar's markup or styles.
- **Checks:** `beat-comic-dock.mjs` (order, icon-only names, Previous and Next targets, Tab order, the dialog, the pinned set, desktop and phone) and `beat-comic-pause.mjs`.

## Method

1. **The song.** Take the page's number from the tracklist. Choose the song from the soundtrack library (`mtw-soundtrack`: open `/m/soundtrack` in MTW Studio, `npm run studio`, http://127.0.0.1:8792) by tempo, feel, stops, ending and the page's character (a page Matt wants "on the dominant side" gets a hard, bass-heavy track); a new song goes through that skill's intake first. The credit reads `Music: <Title> by <Artist>` and links to the track's own page. Copy it to `site/public/audio/<file>.mp3` and record `usedBy` in the library's `sources.json`.
2. **Beat map.** Read the profile's beat map: `bpm`, `offset`, `phrases`, `stops`, `hits`, `endBeat`, `loopBeats`, and the per-bar loudness (drops, breakdowns, the accent on a phrase's last beat). Confirm the grid by ear with `?beat=tick` before writing; if the flash leads or trails the kick, fix `offset` first.
3. **The story.** Use `mtw-articulation` before writing a word, then [story-craft](references/story-craft.md). Tell one story in chapters, not labels; homage, swagger and the turn for a service page; the page's own claims only; no client names, no prices.
4. **Cut it.** Copy [assets/story-template.ts](assets/story-template.ts) to `site/src/data/beat-comics/<slug>.ts` and cut it to the map by the standard above: pages on phrases or half phrases, panels by the clock (1.5 s), slams on accents, freezes on stops, the close at the song's end (`beats` = `loopBeats`, `close` = the close page's start).
5. **Wire it.** Add `<BeatComic story={...} />` after the page's `ScrollReveal`, with `collapseTo` set to the page's hero selector (`.page-art` on a service page). **A Journal entry** is wired by data, not by its page: add its track to Disc 2 in `tracklist.ts` and its story to `journal.ts` by slug; `JournalEntryPage.astro` renders it, with `collapseTo: '.journal-entry-hero .page-title'` and `closeScene.layout: 'full-bleed'`. Journal pages have no scroll reveal, so the fold hands over as soon as it ends (the engine waits for a reveal only when the target takes part in one). Journal files are owned by the Journal session: coordinate through `discussion/handoff/STATUS.md` first.
6. **Verify, in this order.** `beat-comic-check.mjs` until no ERROR; `beat-comic-bench.mjs` and compare every pair with the homepage, fixing each difference; `beat-comic-frames.mjs` at every chapter start, slam and freeze, desktop and phone (and at every switched or re-sourced box, so the cinematic photos' crops read in thirds, quarters and the 4x4 grid under their captions and name bars); `beat-comic-links.mjs` on every page with a grid or "Your turn." images (a named cell with no link, such as a page's own crew cell, must pass); `beat-comic-hold.mjs`, with playback into the hold for every changed close (the resolve timing and the hold before the song file ends); `beat-comic-mixtape.mjs --to <path>` and `beat-comic-autoplay.mjs`; a production build; then play it through with music in a real browser and run `ai-slop-check`. Report what was played, seen and heard; frames are not playback.
7. **Record.** Matt's decisions in the spec; facts in `wiki/site/beat-comic.md`; new craft here and in the Codex edition (identical); each reversal of Matt's in `lessons.md` with what changed, and the comic's beat-by-beat row in `demo-anatomy.md`.
8. **Deploy** when Matt asks: commit the owned files by pathspec, export that commit's `site/` subtree and deploy it with the named-revision procedure in `wiki/site/deployment.md` (never the working tree). Vercel builds one at a time (10-15 minutes each): wait for a running build to be Ready before deploying the next commit, or the older build can land last; deploy the newest commit, which carries everything before it. Smoke-test the page on the review alias and record the deployment.

## Fixed decisions (Matt, 2026-09-26 and 27; history in lessons.md)

- **Music on by default, the whole time.** A first visit's browser refuses audible autoplay until the first click, tap or key: while it does, the credit tag stays open with a pulsing "Tap for sound" and the homepage cover scrolls "CLICK THE SCREEN FOR SOUND" ("TAP" on touch). That sound box shows from its `at` to its `until` (just before the next boxes land) whatever the sound state (Matt, 2026-09-28): it asks while the music is held back or muted, says "SOUND ON" while the music is audible, and while muted a click or tap on the screen during it turns sound on (a click outside it leaves the mute alone). A tap is not a swipe: only a move beyond 12 px enters the page. The track loops through the hold and after the reader has entered the page.
- **One sound setting for the site** ("link scene audio to button click audio"): the nav's Sound Toggle and the credit tag's mute are one state (`localStorage` `mtw-sound`, owned by `tactile.js`); music becoming audible turns it on; muting either mutes both, on every page; while music is held back the nav speaker shows off and pressing it starts the music.
- **The mixtape, mixed like a DJ** ("similar to a dj mixing and fading two jams"): leaving, the track dips under the page transition's cover and hands over; the next page carries it on and fades it out over 4 s while its own track fades in. The engine only observes the page transition's attributes.
- **The credit** is a tag, bottom right, on 59% black, open with an animated equalizer for ten seconds, then folded to a mute button; exact provider title, linked to the track's page.
- **Skip Intro**, Netflix-style, with **Pause/Play** in the toolbar (Matt, 2026-09-28: "for all pages add a pause/play button to the left of the "Skip Intro" button", reversing 2026-09-26; then "remove "Pause" text from button"): Pause/Play is icon only, between Previous track and Next track; it stops and resumes the story clock and the music together, is not a way into the page, and leaves with the stage, as Skip Intro does. A phone shows Skip Intro as its icon; the names stay.
- **The ways in are links** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "link the ways in boxes to their sections, so a user can click through"). Every service cell in a comic's grid carries `href`, its page from the tracklist by the cell's name (never retyped); the "N WAYS IN." cell has none. The "Your turn." images link too ("link those to something on the site"): the homepage and Agentic by the starting-point photo, as the homepage's Starting Points do; a landing page's own questions to its services or the contact form. Such a link covers its panel, is named by the caption, and lights the caption box paper on hover or focus. The component lays real links over the cells outside the aria-hidden scenes; they take a click, tap or focus only while their cell shows, anywhere else the click for sound still lands, and following one plays the page transition and the DJ crossfade. Hover or focus lights the cell's name bar teal-light with ink type and inks its frame teal-light: flat, no new buttons or labels. Run `beat-comic-links.mjs` on every page with a grid.
- **Slabs** hold about 2.6 beats above the cut tiles, then explode into their own pixels; readable sub-lines; crisp borders (no teal fringe; whole-pixel rim and offset; Matt, 2026-09-28).
- **Images shimmer and stay still** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "glimmer pixel by pixel", then "the holo graphics are just animating up and down 1 px it looks like a bug, keep still site wide but add shimmer to pixels"): every image panel, photo or hologram, stays in place; each pixel's opacity dips on its own phase (four levels) and a lit pixel now and then sparkles one palette step brighter for a step (teal to teal-light, teal-light to paper), on the motion clock (still on a freeze and a pause). No nod, no movement of the picture.
- **The Tracklist is the comics' global navigation** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "the homepage tracklist should use the same style as the marquee tracklist on the section pages, i am trying to give this global navigation", "make this a component", "this will be the perm way to stay in the 8-bit experience"). One playlist (`playlist.ts`: Disc 1, every Disc 1 track of `tracklist.ts` (19 with About Matt), each top-level page heading a group (the landing pages their services, About Matt alone); Disc 2, the Journal, its label linked to `/journal/` and its five entries numbered 01-05, every row a link), drawn by one renderer (`drawTracks`, rows as the landing marquee's strips) on the homepage cover and in the Tracklist dialog (`Tracklist.astro`) on every comic. Each disc is a block (its label spanning, its tracks in columns of their own, balanced within the disc), stacked like a double album's back cover: on the homepage cover Disc 1 is 7, 7 and 5 under its label and Disc 2 is 3 and 2 under its. The scale is whole device pixels (a phone's 5/3 CSS px keeps all 26 rows readable where 2 CSS px would not fit). It is 8-bit: the engine's own condensed 3x7 pixel font (an original face drawn for the comic, so no font file or licence) at whole-pixel scale (3 CSS px a font pixel on a desktop, 2 on a phone), the pixel grid over it, notched bars; balanced columns; every row a real link, the page's own track playing and not a link.
- **Let's Talk** (Matt, 2026-09-28: "add a "Let's Talk" button to the left of that in 8 bit please, on all pages"): pixel words on a notched teal-light block, a real link to `/contact/`, left of Tracklist in the dock and pinned with it after the fold.
- **Chapter cards** are crisp flat type on the teal banner, one line, slammed in on real time (a freeze only extends them).
- **No glass and nothing radial** in the motion; SFX, panel art and pixel type are pixelated, never the chapter cards.
- **Reduced motion or `?reel=off`**: no reel; the static hero is the page.
