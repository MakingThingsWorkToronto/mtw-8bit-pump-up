# Beat Comic engine reference

## Shared presentation header

The shared header follows every full-window presentation (Matt, 2026-10-09): comics align logo/navigation to the measured left/right canvas margins; Task Field and Diagram Tour expansion comes from each actual component’s progress. Continuous scroll/fold progress has no extra CSS smoothing; discrete entry/close animates with the presentation. Reduced motion keeps final alignment without animation. Launched presentations contain the actual shared header and existing Close control within the modal, restore the same header on close or before navigation swaps, and preserve Escape/focus return. Verify desktop/phone, activation, resize, exit and reduced motion through the common runtime; never implement a separate per-story/model header.

Panel frames and panel-link focus outlines use the site’s brand teal `#1a9ba1`. Gutters and margins use teal or the existing dark ground, never white/off-white paper (Matt, 2026-10-09). This supersedes older paper-frame/focus wording below; lettering and sprite highlights keep their palette.

What `site/public/scripts/beat-comic.js` does, with its constants, so a story can be written against known behaviour. Change behaviour here and in the engine together; the demo (`demo-anatomy.md`) is the regression reference.

## Clock and loop

- Beat length `B = 60 / story.bpm`. Loop = `story.beats × B`.
- The music is the clock while it plays: `T = (audio.currentTime − music.offset) mod loop`; at the loop end the music restarts on `offset`. Otherwise the page clock runs. When playback starts late (autoplay refused, slow load, first gesture), the music seeks to the story's current time.
- Music: on by default, volume 0.85. A refused start (every first visit) marks the tag `is-waiting`: it stays open with a pulsing "Tap for sound" and retries on the first `pointerdown`, `pointerup`, `touchend`, `click` or `keydown`; the tag folds ten seconds after music actually starts. Muting keeps the music playing silently so the clock holds. Collapse leaves it playing.
- `holdEnd`: the clock stops 0.02 s before the loop end and drawing stops (nothing moves); the still slab stays. If the song file ends first (its `ended` rewinds it) or the music clock jumps back more than a second, the story holds at the end there instead of running from its start (seeks and resyncs reset that guard); `beat-comic-check.mjs` fails a story that ends past its song file. The music keeps looping (the track restarts when it ends), pauses while the tab is hidden and resumes on return, and carries on after collapse, when the sound tag moves to `document.body` as a fixed `.rl-tag--page`.
- Freezes: inside a `freeze` window the **motion clock** holds (embers, fire, camera push, slab boil, chapter-card animation); DOM timing (panels landing, captions) still uses real time.

## Layers (bottom to top)

ground (ink, or the teal band ground) → back canvas (Grid Background, campfire, embers, pen trail, field) → book (pages of panels) → chapter card (z 2) → front canvas (cuts and explosion debris, z 3) → SFX slabs (z 4) → dock (Skip Intro and music tag, z 5) → progress line.

## Panels

- Land at `(page.b + at) × B`: pop from scale 1.07 in 0.1 s; the SVG frame inks round its perimeter in 0.3 s (out-cubic); the back grid lights a trail of tiles behind the pen.
- Pixel art resolves through pixel sizes 56, 40, 28, 20, 14, 10, 8, 6 px, one step every 0.04 s; the final pixel is about panel height ÷ 40 (3 to 6 px; 0.65× on phones). A slam on the same page re-pixelates the page's panels for 0.3 s.
- Images: drawn small (black letterbox), auto-levelled (4th to 97th percentile), square Bayer dither, snapped to ink-panel `#112126`, teal `#1a9ba1`, teal-light `#5fc4c9`, paper `#f2f2ef` at cuts 0.21 / 0.36 / 0.62. The posterized grid is kept per pixel size (never re-decoded or re-posterized per frame).
- Shimmer (2026-09-28; the one-pixel nod of 825c107b was removed the same day: "it looks like a bug, keep still site wide but add shimmer to pixels"), every image panel once resolved: the picture never moves. Each final pixel has a hashed phase (0-63); its opacity dips over 14 of 64 steps to about 0.86, 0.72 and 0.58 (`GL_DIP` 0.42; four levels) over the flat panel colour, odd phases twice as fast; on phase 40-41 a teal pixel sparkles teal-light and a teal-light one paper, for that step only (`SPARK_AT`; paper and the panel colour never sparkle). Steps are 12 a second on the motion clock, each panel offset by a fifth of a step (panel index mod 5) so a grid does not redraw on one frame; only the opacity and the last step's sparkles are rewritten. While a panel resolves or a slam re-pixelates it, its chunky frames show plain. A freeze or a pause holds it; reduced motion has no reel. Render cost: lessons 11, 27 and 37.
- Chat (`chat: true`): a pixel chat window, three alternating bubbles of text bars appearing as the panel lands, then a typing indicator.
- Counting figures (`count`): the pixel type is `fmt` with # = `from` + `step` x floor((beats since landing - `after`) / `every`), capped at `to`, `fmt1` when it is 1; `pix` shows before `after`. The homepage: 14 APPS. +1 every 1.5 beats; 70+ FLOWS. +5 every 1.5 beats; 3 TB. for 1.5 beats, then 1 to 16 UNIQUE MIGRATIONS. every 0.45 beat; 6,000+ from 1,000+ by 250 every 0.2 beat (there in 4 beats); 125+ from 115+ by 1 every 0.3 beat (there in 3). Numbers print with thousands separators.
- Pixel type: Barlow Condensed 700; first word paper, the rest teal-light, flat teal shadow; wraps to two lines when that makes it ≥ 25% bigger (beside the mark too, since 2026-09-28: the homepage cover's MAKING THINGS / WORK. on a phone; a panel whose type is limited by its height never wraps, so the service covers are unchanged); beside the MTW mark in wide panels, below it in tall ones.
- Captions type at 95 characters a second (the second caption at 75), 0.16 s after the panel lands.
- Camera: each page pushes from 1.00 to 1.03 over its length; a panel landing kicks +0.012, a slam +0.03 with a 12 px stepped shake (24 fps) for 0.3 s.

## Slams (SFX)

- Pixel sprite: jagged slab (long edges only), teal-light face, ink rim and offset, paper poster type with ink outline, optional ink subline (0.42 of the word), paper speed lines. Resolves through 30, 20, 13, 9, 6 px (0.6x on phones); a slab with a subline settles at about the subline height divided by 11 (2 px minimum) so it can be read. Crisp borders (2026-09-28): the slab's corners, rim width and offset are whole sprite pixels, and its sprite snaps to ink, teal-light and paper only (no teal), so anti-aliased edges never leave a teal fringe.
- Pops in over 0.18 s (back-out), boils on twos (12 fps), holds `len` beats (default 2.6), then explodes: every sprite pixel flies outward (280-900 px/s, upward bias), falls (gravity 900 px/s²) and fades over 0.62 s.
- Rendered by time on every page, so a slam on a chapter's last beat carries across the cut; its centre is clamped so the whole slab stays on the page.
- `still: true`: no speed lines, no boil, no shake (the freeze-frame gag).

## Pages, cards and cuts

- A page is live from `b` to `b + l`. Its chapter card flashes for 0.95 beat of the motion clock from `b` (a freeze extends it), and never for less than 0.57 s of real time (the homepage's 0.95 beat at 100 BPM; a 165.75 BPM song gave 0.34 s), its slam-in on real time so a freeze never holds it mid-animation: a teal banner strip, the title slams from 1.3× in 0.09 s, the strip opens from 0.4× height in 0.05 s, an outlined echo slides off.
- Cuts: square tiles in the Grid Background pitch cover over 0.4 beat before a page boundary and clear over 0.55 beat after it, swept left-right, bottom-top, right-left, top-bottom or diagonal in turn (never radial); about 10% teal tiles in the ink.
- The corner box shows the MTW mark and "No. n" (chapter number) from one beat into each titled page.
- Portrait screens: a page without a full-width panel is transposed; a panel's `rp` overrides. The close is never transposed.
- A held story (`holdEnd`) never wraps its cuts: the last page does not wipe back towards the cover.
- Chapter titles never wrap: on measure, a title wider than the banner is shrunk to fit one line.
- The disc (2026-09-28): `story.track.disc` above 1 prints on the CD ticker as "DISC 2 * TRACK 01 * TITLE" and on the marquee as "DISC 2 TRACK 01:"; Disc 1's tracks read as before ("TRACK 05 * TITLE").
- CD display panel (`cd: true`, `story.track`): a teal LCD (90% of the panel's width) with an ink shadow; TRACK and TIME labels; seven-segment digits (lit in ink, unlit segments shown faintly in teal-light, like a real LCD), the time as m:ss of the story clock; a PLAY triangle shown on even beats; the title scrolling as CD text along the bottom, clipped to the display. A tall panel (phones) stacks TRACK above TIME.
- Marquee panel (`marquee: true`, `story.track`): "TRACK nn:" in teal-light and the title in paper, pixel type at 62% of the panel height with a teal rule top and bottom, drawn at the panel's pixel size and stepped 12 times a second on the motion clock at 1.1 panel heights a second, repeating seamlessly.
- Scrolling pixel type (`pix` + `scroll: true`, a landing page's cover, 2026-09-28): the MTW mark (62% of the art's height) then the text as a marquee beside it (first word teal-light, the rest paper, ink shadow, teal rules over the strip), type at min(62% of the height, 14% of the width), 1.8 type heights a second, stepped 12 a second on the motion clock; it lands still and reads for two beats of the motion clock, then runs. A tall panel (phone) puts the mark above and the strip across the lower third (type up to 24% of the width). With `ticker`, the CD strip stays below. Drawn by `strip()`, the marquee strip renderer the tracklist rows share.
- Marquee strip (`strip()`, 2026-09-28): teal rules over and under the strip, the first word teal-light and the rest paper, a flat ink shadow; given a run time it repeats and slides left at its speed. In Barlow Condensed (the landing covers' names, unchanged to the pixel) or in the pixel font (`pf`: the tracklist rows, whose rules go over the shadow).
- Pixel font (2026-09-28): an original condensed face drawn for the comic: capitals, digits and the titles' punctuation (. , - & : ' ! ? / + a bullet and a play arrow), 7 pixels tall, most glyphs 3 wide (I 1, N 4, M and W 5), 1 pixel between glyphs, a space 2; `pfDraw` fills one rectangle per pixel at whole canvas pixels, never anti-aliased. It is for type too small for pixelated Barlow to read (under about ten pixels to the em): the tracklist rows and the dock's pixel labels (Let's Talk, the dialog's TRACKLIST. and CLOSE X: `canvas.rl-px[data-px]`, 2 CSS px a font pixel, the heading at the list's scale). No font file, so nothing to load or license.
- Tracklist panel (`tracks`, the homepage cover, 2026-09-28): drawn at one font pixel per canvas pixel into its own buffer (the panel's final pixel is the layout's scale, exactly, even when it is a fraction of a CSS px; the chunky resolve frames are that buffer drawn small), the pixel grid over it as on every panel. Layout (`trackLayout`, Disc 2 version): the list is one **block per disc**, stacked: the disc label spans the block, then the disc's tracks in columns of their own. Within a disc, columns break only between groups: a landing track starts a group (Disc 1); in a disc without landing tracks (the Journal) every track is a group, joined without a gap. It tries every scale from 5 to 1 CSS px **in steps of one device pixel** (1-5 CSS px on a desktop; 5/3, 2, 7/3 ... on a 3x phone), row heights 12 down to 9 font pixels with gaps of 3 between groups (2, then 1, at row height 9), padding 2% (3-6) and columns 2% apart; for each disc and each column count (1-4) it keeps the most even cut (equal evenness: the earlier columns fuller) whose names fit, at most one name per disc overflowing its column (scale 1 takes any); then every combination of the discs' column counts that fits the height. The score is the cap height in CSS px times (1 - the largest spread between a disc's tallest and shortest column), plus a hundredth of the row height, less a thousandth per column. The homepage cover on a desktop: 3 CSS px, rows 9; Disc 1 in three columns of 7, 7 and 5 (01-07 / 08-14 / 15-19), Disc 2 in two of 3 and 2; Information Governance and the rubric title scroll. On a 3x phone: 5/3 CSS px, rows 9, one column of 26 rows (at 1 device px a CSS px the phone falls to 1 CSS px). The Tracklist dialog on a desktop: 3 CSS px, rows 12, Disc 1 in 7, 7 and 5, Disc 2 in one column of 5; on a 3x phone 5/3 CSS px, rows 12, one column. Row styles: a disc label and a landing track are strips with teal rules, the first word teal-light and the rest paper; a service is dimmer (number teal, name teal-light); a note is teal; the playing row waits cued (a notched teal-light outline, a still play arrow) until `live`, then is a notched teal-light bar with ink words, the arrow blinking on the beat and the song's time at its right; hover or keyboard focus lights a row as a paper bar; the newest row flashes as a solid bar for one step. Rows land one every 0.2 beat from 0.16 s after the panel; a name longer than its column scrolls as the marquee does (still for two beats, then 18 font pixels a second, stepped 12 a second). Each row is cached as its own sprite and redrawn only when its state changes (lit, cued, the blink, the clock's second, the scroll step), so a scrolling name repaints one row (lesson 37).
- Tracklist row links: one `a.rl-link.rl-link--row[data-panel][data-row]` per row with an `href`, named "Track nn: title" on Disc 1 and "Disc 2, Track nn: title" after it (`trackLabel`; a disc label by its title), its words invisible, placed over its row in px (the panel's offset plus the row box) and riding the page camera; live from `tracks.live` (after the sound box: the checker fails it earlier) and once the row has landed, until the cut before the page ends; hover or focus sets the panel's `data-hot` to the row.

## Back canvas

- Grid pitch = window width ÷ 58, clamped to 18-28 px; tiles 76% of pitch; the site's Grid Background tile looks and shimmer.
- Campfire: the bottom 32% of rows glows, flickering per column and pulsing on each beat; embers (0.7 per column) rise, drift and fade over 2.4-5 s. `ember` scales it.
- Field (`field: true`): stalks grow from the bottom over 1.3 s and sway; paper-dart sprites (5) launch from `planes` beats in, trailing teal-light tiles.
- The teal ground swaps tile colours to ink and white.

## Skip, collapse and input

- The motion toolbar (2026-09-28; `MotionToolbar.astro`, `motion-toolbar.js`, `motion-toolbar.css`, canonical for every motion experience): left to right Let's Talk (`a.rl-talk`, `/contact/`, pixel words on a notched teal-light block, paper on hover), Tracklist (`button.rl-list`, Skip Intro's style with a pixel list icon), Previous track (`a.rl-prev`), Pause/Play (`button.rl-pause`), Next track (`a.rl-next`) (the three icon only, 36 px, pixel icons from the pixel font's glyphs at 2 CSS px a pixel, named by aria-label and title), Skip Intro (`button.rl-skip`; a phone shows a pixel double-chevron), the music tag (the engine's, the component's slot). The engine registers with `MTWToolbar` (its beat, the song time, `setPaused` for Pause/Play, `collapse` for Skip Intro) and reflects its pause state with `MTWToolbar.setPaused`. On collapse, `MTWToolbar.pin(tag)` moves Let's Talk, Tracklist, Previous, Next and the tag into `body > .rl-pin`, fixed bottom right (the tag keeps `.rl-tag--page`). The pixel font, `strip()`, `trackLayout`, `trackState` and `drawTracks` now live in `motion-toolbar.js` (`window.MTWPixel`), which loads first; without it the engine releases the page.
- The Tracklist dialog (`#rl-tracklist`, `Tracklist.astro`, rendered by the toolbar and moved to `<body>` so it works after the fold): the page's `#mtw-playlist` (the toolbar's JSON from `playlist.ts`, with this page's track playing) painted by `drawTracks` into `.rl-tl__px`, always live, rows landing every 0.03 s, laid out from the top in the room the dialog allows and then shrunk to its rows; real links (`a.rl-tl__a`) over the rows with invisible words, the playing row and the Journal note as `p.rl-tl__a--still` ("Now playing: ..."). Opening moves focus to the first row; Tab and the arrow keys walk the rows and Close without leaving the dialog; Escape, Close or a click on the scrim closes it and focus returns to the button (`aria-expanded` follows). While it is open the comic and its music play on, in sync (nothing pauses: pausing the story alone would part it from the music), and no wheel, touch move, scroll key or scroll folds the comic; `html.rl-tl-open` stops the page scrolling behind it. A pointer opening focuses the first row without lighting it; keyboard focus lights it. Following a row is an ordinary link: the page transition and the DJ crossfade.
- The toolbar's buttons slide in after 1.2 s; the music tag opens with its equalizer, folds to the mute button after 10 s, and reopens on hover or focus.
- Pause/Play (`.rl-pause`, left of Skip Intro, 2026-09-28): Pause takes `T` from the clock, sets `state = 'paused'`, stops drawing and pauses the music; Play sets the page clock to `T`, seeks the music to `offset + T` and plays it (or starts held-back music: Play's click is the gesture; Pause's never starts it), and restarts the held embers where they stopped. The label says the next action ("Pause" / "Play"; icon only on a phone, the label its accessible name); the tag's equalizer rests while paused. A paused comic stays up; a scroll, key, swipe or Skip Intro still enters the page, and the music then plays on into it. A hidden tab does not resume a paused comic.
- The first wheel, touch-move, scroll key (not in a form control), Skip Intro, or any script scroll collapses: the scenes blur out over 0.28 s while the stage clips to `collapseTo`'s resting box over 0.95 s; the scroll reveal is released at 0.42 s; the framed grid holds until the real panel begins its reveal (at most 4 s), then fades over 0.55 s. It waits for the reveal only when there is one (2026-09-28): the page runs the scroll reveal (`html[data-reveal-armed]`, set by `ScrollReveal.astro`) and the target carries `data-reveal` or `data-reveal-type`. Otherwise (a Journal entry, whose `.journal-entry-hero .page-title` never reveals) it hands over when the fold ends: about 1.5 s from the scroll to `data-reel="done"` instead of up to 5.5 s. Wheel inertia is swallowed until the gesture ends.
- Before first paint the component sets `data-reel` and holds the scroll reveal; with reduced motion or `?reel=off` it does nothing. If the engine has not started 1.5 s after `load`, the page is released.

## The mixtape (between pages)

- Departure: a `MutationObserver` on `<html data-mtw-leaving>` (set by the page transition when a link is followed) fades the music to silence over 0.6 s (quadratic) and pauses it, inside the transition's 660 ms cover.
- Arrival: while `<html data-mtw-nav>` is set and the `.page-transition` layer's `data-phase` is not `reveal`, the story renders its first frame and waits; it starts its clock and music when the reveal begins (observed on both attributes; a 3.5 s fallback never leaves it waiting).
- `sessionStorage` `mtw-mixtape` = `{ unlocked, track }`: `unlocked` records that the visitor has already given the gesture. Mute is not session state: it is the site's sound setting.

## DJ crossfade

- Leaving (`data-mtw-leaving`): if not muted, `mtw-mixtape.tail` = `{ src, t, at, vol }`; the track ramps to 60% of its volume over 0.6 s (sine) and plays on until the page unloads.
- Arriving (`begin()` sees a tail): the story's track starts at volume 0; once it plays it ramps to 0.85 over 4 s (sine), and a second `Audio` plays the tail's track from `t` plus the time since `at`, ramping from its handed-over volume to 0 over 4 s (1 - cos), then pauses. A tail older than 8 s, of the same track, or while muted is dropped.

## The close (every comic)

- Parts land in order: the heading's lines on close beats 0 and 1.5 (one flowing heading on 0 in a full-bleed close), the lede on 3, each popping in from 1.14x over 0.12 s; the image (or the homepage's panel, or the full-bleed image) fades in over one beat from half a beat after the type is smooth (`ART_AT`; beat 4 when there is no `pixelate`). A hologram's figure switches on and its `img` fades, since an opacity on the figure would isolate its `mix-blend-mode: screen` and show the black box while fading (lesson 5).
- `closeScene.pixelate`, **through the ages** (2026-09-28): the heading's words are split into spans (`.rl-hw`, keeping the accent span's colour), and each heading gets a canvas (`.rl-hero-px`) while its own text goes transparent (`.is-px`). Stages at `PX_EM` = 16, 28 and 48 pixels to the em (8-bit, 16-bit, the familiar screen pixels): stage 0 from the landing until the `pixelate` beat (`AT0`; `true` = 5), stage 1 on `AT0`, stage 2 on `AT0 + 1`, the page's own smooth type on `AT0 + 2` (`SMOOTH_AT`), each a hard cut on the beat. Every stage draws each word at its own span's place (`offsetLeft`, `offsetTop` + the font's ascent), in the word's own colour, with a 1-bit edge (alpha 110 or more becomes opaque, the rest clear): no palette snap, no fringe, and the cut to smooth type moves nothing. The pixel is at least 1 CSS px, so a small heading's familiar stage is aliased type at 1 px. Each stage is drawn once (keyed by pixel size and box).
- `closeScene.layout: 'full-bleed'` (a Journal entry, About Matt; 2026-09-28): the close carries `figure.rl-close__bleed` (the hero image, `object-fit: cover`, no blend, with the page hero's ink gradient: from the left on a desktop, from the bottom at 850 px and below) under one flowing heading (`lines[0] <span class="accent">lines[1]</span>`, as the page's h1). On every measure (`placeBleed`), the engine finds the page's own hero `img` by the story image's `src` and gives the figure its bounding box and its computed `object-position` and `object-fit` (so the crop is the page's, and a phone's shorter hero stays short), and puts the text block at the page title's box (the h1's parent) with the h1's font size, line height, letter spacing, weight and balanced wrapping, and the lede's size, line height, margin and colour. Fallbacks in CSS: the image under the header edge to edge, the text 640 px wide at `clamp(52px, 5.6vw, 88px)`.
- `holdLive`: once held, the back canvas keeps drawing at `END + time since the hold`, so the embers keep rising; nothing else moves.

## Ways in (service links)

- Markup: `nav.rl-links` (aria-label "Services"), a sibling of the scenes and outside their `aria-hidden`, at z 3 with the book's box; one `.rl-links__page[data-page]` per page with links and one `a.rl-link[data-panel]` per panel with `href`, its text the panel's `name` (transparent, laid over the cell's name bar), or for a panel without a name (`a.rl-link--cap`, the "Your turn." images) its `cap`, visually hidden. The layer takes no pointer, so a click anywhere but a live link is still the stage's (the click for sound).
- The engine gives each link its panel's `--x/--y/--w/--h` (portrait transposition and `rp` included) and the page layer the page's own camera transform (push, kick, shake), so links stay on their cells within a pixel.
- Live (`.is-on`: visible, takes the pointer and focus) from 0.12 s after its panel lands (the name bar) until its `until` or the cut cover before its page ends (0.4 beat early), whichever is first; otherwise `visibility: hidden`. Hidden while collapsing.
- Hover or `:focus-visible`: the name bar teal-light with ink type, a `--rl-line` teal-light frame inside the cell; focus adds a 2 px brand-teal outline. The engine also marks the panel `.is-hot` on the link's hover or focus, which turns a caption box paper (the linked panel without a name bar).
- A click, tap or key on a link does not start this page's music (it is leaving); the page transition's `data-mtw-leaving` then fades and hands over the track as for any link.

## Site sound (with `tactile.js`)

- Mute starts from `localStorage` `mtw-sound` (`off` = muted; unset or `on` = music on) or `?mute`.
- When music becomes audible the engine calls `MTWTactile.setSound(true)`; muting calls `setSound(false)`. `tactile.js` announces every change as a window `mtw-sound` event, which the engine follows (the nav toggle's click is a gesture, so it can start held-back music).
- While the browser holds the music back the engine calls `MTWTactile.setBlocked(true)`: the Sound Toggle shows off, and pressing it asks for sound rather than turning it off. `setBlocked(false)` once the music plays.
- Sound prompt panel (`sound: true`): the marquee renderer with "CLICK" ("TAP" under `pointer: coarse`) in teal-light and "THE SCREEN FOR SOUND"; shown from `at` to `until` in every sound state: the prompt while the music is held back or muted, "SOUND" (teal-light) "ON" while it is audible (`data-say` "prompt" or "on" on the panel). Muted, a click or `touchend` (not a swipe) on the stage outside the dock while it shows calls `setMuted(false)`. `until` (any panel): hidden from that page beat. Marquee type is 62% of the strip's height, at most a seventh of its width, scrolling at 1.8 type heights a second.

## High Scores (with `holdEnd`)

- Markup: `nav.rl-scores` (outside the aria-hidden scenes; real links), one `li > a` per entry with `data-score`; hidden until the hold. The links' text is transparent; a canvas behind them paints the board at 2 px pixels (ink board, 4 px teal-light rim, flat teal offset, "HIGH SCORES" as poster type, rows in pixel type upper case, each fitted to its row), redrawn at most 20 times a second when something changes. Hover or focus lights a row teal-light with ink text; a bumped score flashes its row teal.
- `center: true` (the homepage): the board is centred horizontally and in the space between the held slab and the dock, and the close's heading fades out. Otherwise, on the first held frame, the close's `.rl-close__art` or `.rl-close__panel` fades out and the board takes its box (desktop: right-aligned to it, at least 440 px wide, vertically centred and kept below the top slab, from 40% of the height; phone: full width at the art's top, with two or three rows, as many as fit above the dock).
- Every beat: one entry (three in four from the visible page) gains 1, 7, 42, 99, 404, 1337, 9001 or 31337, doubled one time in twelve, modulo 10,000,000; the number rolls over 0.45 s and the row flashes teal in three steps; the board re-sorts and re-ranks (1ST, 2ND ...). Every six beats it pages on (always on the homepage; on a service page only when its rows do not fit).
- Collapse stops the board; it fades with the stage.

## Review aids

`?t=<s>` seek, `?hold` freeze, `?mute`, `?slow=<n>` slow collapse, `?beat` metronome (`?beat=tick` clicks). `window.MTWBeatComic.status()` and `.seek(t)`.
